# Buyer interview guide (v0.2)

**Goal:** Hear how real people described what they wanted *before* they knew which car to buy, and where online search helped or failed them. These interviews test the intent map from 14 blog letters against everyday buyers.

**Who:** 5 people who bought or seriously shopped for a car in the last 12 months. Aim for a mix: at least one family buyer, one first-time or young buyer, one used-car buyer, and one who financed or leased. Friends, coworkers and neighbors are fine, but not other product managers.

**Format:** 20 minutes, video or in person. Ask permission to take notes, and don't record without consent. Write down their exact words where you can; those phrases become test queries.

---

## Opening (2 min)
"I'm researching how people search for cars online. There are no right answers. I'm interested in what actually happened, not what should have happened."

## 1. The trigger (3 min)
- What made you start looking for a car?
- Who else had a say in the decision?

## 2. The first description (5 min) — the most important part
- Before you looked at any listings, how would you have described what you needed? Say it the way you'd say it to a friend.
- What did you know you *didn't* want?
- Did you have a specific model in mind at the start? If so, where did that come from?

*Listen for:* use cases ("fit two car seats"), exclusions, reference cars ("like my sister's RAV4"), subjective words ("reliable", "not boring").

## 3. The search (6 min)
- Walk me through the first time you searched online. What site, and what did you type or click first?
- Was there a moment the results felt wrong or useless? What did you do next?
- Did you ever search by monthly payment instead of price? How did that go?
- What did you have to look up somewhere else (reviews, specs, YouTube, a friend)?

*Listen for:* rephrasing, giving up on the search box and switching to filters, zero results, facts they couldn't find on the listing.

## 4. The decision (3 min)
- What finally made you choose the car you got, or stop looking?
- If a search tool could have asked you one question at the start, what should it have asked?

## Close (1 min)
"Is there anything about shopping for a car online that annoyed you that I didn't ask about?"
